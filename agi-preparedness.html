Section 1 — My Thesis

What I mean by "AGI is here"

When I say "AGI," I'm not making a philosophical claim about consciousness. I mean something more practical:

The cost and availability of general-purpose cognitive work is collapsing fast enough to change how companies, markets, and institutions behave.

That doesn't require magic. It requires capability that is:

  • Good enough
  • Cheap enough
  • Reliable enough
  • Integrated enough

…to become default infrastructure.

The core claim

If intelligence becomes abundant, returns concentrate around:

  1. Compute and infrastructure (the "picks and shovels")
  2. Energy and physical constraints (power, cooling, networks)
  3. Distribution and integration (who ships it into real workflows)
  4. Coordination (policy, labor, trust, institutions lag capability)

This is why I'm less interested in "cool AI apps" and more interested in the stack beneath them.

What I'm not claiming

  • I'm not claiming a specific AGI timeline.
  • I'm not claiming markets will go up forever.
  • I'm not claiming society collapses.
  • I'm not claiming any particular company "wins."
  • I'm not claiming I can outsmart everyone.

I'm claiming the asymmetry is large enough that preparedness is rational.

Scenarios I keep in mind
Base case

Rapid diffusion + messy integration. Big winners, big disruption, lots of lag.

Fast case

Capability jumps faster than institutions adapt; drawdowns + policy shocks likely.

Messy case

Plateaus and leaps; hype cycles; uneven adoption by sector and country.

You can disagree with me here

You might think the timeline is much longer, that current AI is overrated, or that the "returns to intelligence" framing is wrong. I'd love to hear that case—especially if you've thought hard about it.

Section 2 — My Plan (2026 Operating Plan)

My number-one goal

Be prepared to take maximum advantage of AGI-level capability and limit downside—given my resources, network, and insight.

Prepared, to me, means:

  • I'm not cash-starved
  • I'm not over-levered
  • I'm not trapped by a single point of failure
  • I'm building and learning in the real world
  • I'm legible to the right people

The three priorities

  1. Liquidity + optionality
  2. A rules-based AGI exposure (upside capture without constant tinkering)
  3. Public positioning through high-signal work (not hype)

My rules (simple, durable)

Capital rules

  • Hold a minimum runway that makes me calm.
  • Pre-commit to allocation rules before volatility.
  • Avoid leverage that can force sales in a drawdown.

Attention rules

  • Few projects, high learning.
  • Build things that touch real users.
  • Ignore most discourse; track real deployment signals.

Health rules

  • Fitness and sleep are infrastructure.
  • I don't get to "think my way" out of stress forever.

Checklist: "Am I prepared?"

Triggers that would change my plan
  • Clear evidence of long-term capability plateau (not a normal hype cycle)
  • Major regulatory constraint that meaningfully limits deployment
  • A personal/family constraint that increases fragility
  • A better framework that explains the world with fewer assumptions

You can disagree with me here

Maybe you think rules-based investing is naive, or that "liquidity" is just disguised fear. Push back on the strategy itself—I'm listening.

Section 3 — My Hedges

What I'm hedging against

Not "robots take over." The realistic risk is:

Capability accelerates faster than coordination.

That creates turbulence:

  • Market drawdowns
  • Policy shocks
  • Labor repricing
  • Institutional lag and missteps

What still has value when narratives break

I think hedges should be categories, not bets:

  1. Sovereign-agnostic stores of value
  2. Energy and energy control
  3. Real assets tied to non-discretionary needs (food, water, shelter, healthcare delivery)
  4. Jurisdictional optionality (ability to adapt across policy regimes)
  5. Dry powder (optionality to act in dislocations)

The point isn't to "opt out." It's to be unforced.

Failure modes (so I don't fool myself)
  • Gold can be dead money for long stretches.
  • Bitcoin depends on social and regulatory tolerance.
  • Real assets can be illiquid and politicized.
  • Energy plays can be policy-dependent.
  • Cash can decay in real terms.

I'm okay with those tradeoffs if they buy optionality under stress.

Counterarguments I respect
  • "Hedges are a tax on returns." Fair. If the bull case plays out cleanly, I'll underperform the maximally-long crowd.
  • "You can't hedge tail risk; just accept it." Maybe. But I'd rather have options than not.
  • "Jurisdictional optionality is a fantasy for most people." Partly true—but even partial optionality beats none.
